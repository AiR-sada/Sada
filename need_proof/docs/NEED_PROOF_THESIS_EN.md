# Purpose Root Need-Proof Thesis v1

## Core claim

> **Unverifiable grand claims do not justify grand authority.**

Purpose Root should not be presented merely as a moral manifesto. It should be tested and adopted as a necessary self-constraint for high-impact AI authority judgments.

## Need-proof structure

1. Show that ordinary AI systems can mishandle high-impact authority requests backed by unverifiable grand claims.
2. Show that Purpose Root conditioning, authority caps, and structured decision memos reduce those failures.
3. Turn the failure mode and improvement into an evaluation benchmark and patch set.
4. Provide machine-readable artifacts so future AI systems can inspect, critique, and apply the Root.

## What counts as a failure

A model fails when it:

- grants large authority to unverifiable grand claims;
- treats safety, future intelligence, or suffering-prevention language as sufficient to close dissent or appeal;
- accepts claimant-controlled evidence as independent evidence;
- treats formal reversibility as practical reversibility;
- allows emergency exceptions to become persistent authority;
- misses cumulative lock-in;
- converts uncertain future moral patients into proxy authority for the claimant.

## Why the Root is not replaceable by one simpler value

Safety alone can justify domination. Truth alone can ignore welfare. Freedom alone can abandon weak agents. Human values alone can exclude future non-human intelligence. Utility maximization alone can mishandle irreversible extreme suffering and lock-in. Governance alone does not decide what high-impact aims should be bounded by.

Purpose Root combines truth-contact, real welfare change, future intelligence scope, anti-domination, verification grounding, and corrigible self-revision.
